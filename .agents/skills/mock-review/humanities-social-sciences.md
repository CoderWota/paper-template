# Humanities and social sciences review

Apply this reference to relevant claims in interdisciplinary work too. Use the general form's finding rules and scales; this reference adds no score or weighting system.

## Evidence suited to the research question

Judge material and reasoning within the research tradition, ethical access, and actual claims. Random assignment, natural experiments, precise causal effects, large samples, significance, and control of every confounder are not universal requirements. Bounded associations and interpretations can make publishable contributions.

- **Quantitative observational work:** assess measurement, selection, uncertainty, model assumptions, and sensitivity relevant to the claim. Imprecision may establish uncertainty rather than absence of an effect.
- **Qualitative work:** assess case/participant selection, interpretation, researcher position where relevant, and contrary cases. Do not require statistical representativeness or a fixed sample independently of the question.
- **Historical/textual work:** assess provenance, source selection, context, close reading, and competing interpretations. Explain archival gaps by their effects; do not require experimental replication.
- **Conceptual/normative work:** assess concepts, premises, inference, and material objections. Check empirical premises without demanding causal estimates to establish a conceptual or normative argument.

A causal claim may need narrowing while a descriptive, theoretical, or interpretive contribution survives. Request new identification or evidence only when needed for the retained contribution. Feasibility constraints do not excuse fabrication, invalid analysis, or a decisive evidential gap. Checkability means a traceable route through material and reasoning, not identical interpretations or public release of sensitive sources.

## Academic and theoretical contribution

Apply the form's close-work comparison. Recognize new regions, groups, periods, and corpora when they test a boundary, reveal excluded experience, correct an account, separate outcomes, or provide useful replication. Do not force theoretical novelty onto every article type.

For a theoretical contribution, identify a supported change to a concept, relationship, explanation, assumption, boundary, or framework. Clarification, integration, exposing tensions, and historical reinterpretation can qualify. Naming a theory, adding a variable, or listing gaps alone does not establish an advance. No entirely new theory or mechanism is required unless the paper's contribution depends on it.

## Societal contribution

The researcher's confirmed project threshold requires a defensible societal contribution, evaluated separately from academic merit. Trace **social problem → affected people/communities → this paper's added understanding → path to benefit → conditions and limits**. Support the connection with the paper and inspected field literature; label reviewer inference. A topic's importance or generic policy sentence alone does not establish the paper's contribution.

Paths can concern policy, services, education, public debate, cultural understanding, overlooked experiences, or capacity building. Better understanding or reframing can qualify without an intervention. Consider excluded beneficiaries and plausible harms where relevant; do not impose a preferred political conclusion.

Report the applicable evidence state:

- **Observed effect:** checked evidence supports an actual change and a path to benefit. Uptake or attention alone does not prove benefit; do not infer sole causation.
- **Supported potential pathway:** evidence connects the contribution to a concrete use or improved understanding, with untested steps and conditions identified. This satisfies the project threshold without policy adoption or measured intervention effects.
- **Not established:** an identified substantive gap leaves only an asserted benefit. Explain the gap and minimum repair.
- **Not assessed:** decisive material is unavailable. Name the missing check rather than treat access limits as failure.

Societal contribution belongs in the publication forecast. Explain how its presence, absence, or strength matters to the target journal, article type, and claimed contribution. Where the venue requires practical or societal significance, an unmet requirement can be a decisive barrier. Do not apply one journal's expectations to all HSS work or erase strong academic value because direct policy effects were not measured.

Keep the project threshold conclusion explicit. If unmet, do not claim that all project contribution requirements are satisfied. That conclusion is not an automatic rejection gate for a journal with different criteria: preserve the venue-based recommendation, official fields, and forecasts, and explain any difference. A missing explanation supported by existing evidence can need clarification; substantive synthesis or new evidence may need more work. Do not require new fieldwork solely to embellish an impact statement.

## Guidance basis

Official sources inspected on 2026-10-30 inform these distinctions, not universal journal criteria:

- [ESRC impact guidance](https://www.ukri.org/councils/esrc/impact-toolkit-for-economic-and-social-sciences/defining-impact/): academic versus societal impact, including conceptual and capacity-building pathways.
- [Academy of Management reviewer resources](https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/author-and-reviewer-resources/reviewer-resources/): theoretical and empirical contribution, judged with the research approach.
- [AHR scope](https://www.historians.org/news-publications/american-historical-review/about-ahr/): historical significance, interpretation, analysis, and source research.

The potential-pathway threshold and discipline-sensitive standards implement the researcher's confirmed requirements; they do not claim that every journal demands an immediate practical solution.
