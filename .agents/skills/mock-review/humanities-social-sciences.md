# Humanities and social sciences review

Read for a humanities or social sciences work, including an interdisciplinary work's relevant claims. Give every reviewer this guidance. Apply the general form's finding assessment; this reference adds no numeric scale or weighted total.

## Evidence appropriate to the question

Judge whether the material and reasoning support the actual claim within the research tradition, available sources, and ethical access conditions. Do not set natural-science experimental precision as a universal standard. Natural experiments, random assignment, precise causal effects, large samples, statistical significance, and control of every confounder are not general requirements. Narrow scope, imprecise estimates, or inability to identify causation alone do not justify a lower soundness score.

Use the checks that matter for the work:

- Quantitative observational work: measurement, selection, uncertainty, model assumptions, and sensitivity relevant to the stated claim. Associations, patterns, and bounded comparisons can contribute without causal identification. An imprecise estimate may support uncertainty about an effect rather than its absence; assess magnitude claims accordingly.
- Qualitative work: fit of participants or cases to the question, how material was selected and interpreted, researcher position where relevant, and treatment of contrary cases. Do not require statistical representativeness or a fixed sample size independently of the claim.
- Historical or textual work: source provenance and selection, context, close reading, and competing interpretations. Archival gaps or nonrepeatable access need an explanation of their effect, not an automatic demand for experimental replication.
- Conceptual or normative work: clear concepts and premises, defensible inference, and engagement with material objections. Do not demand empirical causal estimates to establish a conceptual distinction or normative argument; check any empirical premises on which it depends.

Distinguish reasonable uncertainty from a demonstrated defect. Explain which claim a limitation affects and how much. Where explicit causal claims exceed the design, first ask what descriptive, interpretive, or theoretical contribution survives narrower wording. Require new identification or evidence only if the central contribution depends on that causal claim. Feasibility constraints do not excuse fabrication, clearly invalid analysis, or a decisive break between evidence and conclusion; social importance does not repair these defects.

For ability to check the work, assess a traceable route from sources through selection and interpretation to conclusions. Identical outcomes across researchers and public release of sensitive material are not universal requirements.

## Literature basis for contribution judgments

Apply the general form's all-discipline literature rules and the skill's read-only handoff boundaries. For academic value, compare what related studies explain and what changes here. For societal value, inspect literature on the concrete problem, affected people, and plausibility or limits of the proposed pathway. Relevant official data or practice evidence can supplement field literature. Literature may support a problem and pathway without demonstrating that this particular paper has already produced a benefit.

## Added knowledge and theoretical contribution

Compare the closest inspected work: what it established, what question or tension remains, what this paper adds, and why that difference changes understanding. Include relevant contrary work; state literature coverage and access limits. Do not infer a gap from an author's assertion or an unsuccessful search. Insufficient comparison leaves originality unassessed rather than low by default.

A new region, group, period, or corpus can reveal an overlooked experience, correct an account, test a boundary, or provide useful replication. Explain that payoff; a changed setting alone does not establish it. Recognize empirical and interpretive contributions without forcing theoretical novelty onto every article type.

For claimed theoretical contributions, identify the change to a concept, relationship, explanation, assumption, boundary, or framework and its support. Contributions can clarify concepts, revise explanations, integrate theories, expose tensions, challenge assumptions, or offer a new historical interpretation. Naming a theory, adding a variable, or listing literature gaps is not itself a theoretical advance. Do not require an entirely new theory or a causal mechanism where neither is needed for the contribution.

## Societal contribution as a required threshold

This skill requires a defensible societal contribution for humanities and social sciences assessments. It is a researcher-confirmed assessment requirement, not a claim about all journals. Assess it separately from academic merit and reference value.

Trace **social problem → affected people or communities → this paper's added understanding → path to benefit → conditions and limits**. Locate support in the paper or inspected evidence; label reviewer inferences and unresolved assumptions. A topic's urgency or a generic sentence about policy implications does not establish the paper's contribution.

Paths may concern policy, services, education, public debate, cultural understanding, representation of overlooked experiences, or capacity building. Helping people understand or reframe a concrete social problem can qualify without proposing an intervention. Consider whose account of the problem is used, who may benefit or be excluded, and plausible harms or tradeoffs where relevant. Do not impose the reviewer's preferred political conclusion.

Report one of these evidence states:

- **Observed effect:** inspected evidence supports an actual change; distinguish uptake or engagement from demonstrated benefit and avoid claiming sole causation without support. Uptake or engagement alone does not meet the threshold unless a supported path to benefit is also established.
- **Supported potential pathway:** a concrete, evidence-supported connection from this paper's contribution to a societal use or better understanding; identify conditions and untested steps. This meets the threshold without policy adoption or measured intervention effects.
- **Not established:** inspected material provides only an asserted benefit, or the proposed connection has an identified substantive gap. Name the gap and repair.
- **Not assessed:** unavailable material prevents judgment. Name the missing check; do not treat missing access as a failed threshold.

## Recommendation and reporting

Each review states added knowledge, theoretical or interpretive contribution where relevant, societal pathway and evidence state, and what survives correction. The meta-review rechecks decisive comparisons and pathway evidence, not just methodological objections. Keep the existing significance anchor and explain academic and societal value separately; strong academic value must not hide an unmet societal threshold, nor must an unmet threshold erase academic value or lower unrelated criteria.

Do not give an unqualified positive overall judgment under this skill when the threshold is not established. Determine repair using the existing treatment boundaries: a missing explanation supported by existing analysis may need only clarification; substantive synthesis or reanalysis needs major revision; new essential material or reconstruction needs reassessment. Do not prescribe new fieldwork just to decorate an otherwise unsupported impact statement. Missing societal contribution alone is not a verified integrity problem or a reason for strong rejection. If decisive material is unavailable, leave the threshold unresolved and give conditional outcomes.

With an official venue form, retain its exact fields, anchors, and venue-based recommendation, including qualitative-only forms. Apply the same substantive assessment standards to an author's self-check, a published paper, and a formal reviewer role, subject to the venue's actual criteria. Report the skill's threshold conclusion separately; do not present it as the venue's rule or silently change its scores. For reading/citation assessment, explain defensible uses even when the threshold is unmet. Report quality, venue treatment, threshold status, and reference value as distinct judgments.

## Basis and limits of the guidance

Official sources checked on 2026-10-30; these inform the distinctions above, not a universal journal standard:

- [ESRC, Defining impact](https://www.ukri.org/councils/esrc/impact-toolkit-for-economic-and-social-sciences/defining-impact/): distinguishes academic and societal impact and recognizes instrumental, conceptual, and capacity-building contributions. The skill also accepts supported potential pathways; it does not label them demonstrated impact.
- [Academy of Management, Reviewer Resources](https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/author-and-reviewer-resources/reviewer-resources/), especially AMJ and AMD guidance: separates theoretical and empirical contributions and relates methodological judgment to the research approach. Management-journal expectations do not govern every humanities or social sciences article.
- [American Historical Association, About the AHR](https://www.historians.org/news-publications/american-historical-review/about-ahr/), criteria for an AHR article: recognizes historical significance and interpretation alongside analysis and deep research. This supports discipline-sensitive assessment, not an AHR-level threshold for every work.

The societal threshold and tolerance of noncausal, appropriately bounded research implement the researcher's confirmed requirements. These sources do not establish a universal obligation to solve an immediate social problem.
