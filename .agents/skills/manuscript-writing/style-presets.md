# Style Presets

A style preset is a set of optional writing choices for one scope of the manuscript: a section, the whole text, or the captions. No preset applies by default.

## Rules for presets

- Use a preset only when the researcher names it, or chooses it after you offer it, for the scope they name.
- Record the choice with `constraint-maintenance`, quoting the researcher's words, so that it becomes a confirmed rule for that scope.
- A venue requirement or the venue's output profile overrides a preset.
- Adopting a preset changes no manuscript text by itself. List the passages it affects, and ask what to do with them.

## Formal English article prose

- Write full forms, not contractions ("do not", not "don't").
- Use quotation marks only around quoted words.
- Use no bold or italic emphasis in running text.
- Replace each em dash with a comma, parentheses, or a clause. Keep a dash only where the researcher chooses to keep it.
- Avoid the possessive 's with a method, model, or system name. Write "the accuracy of METHOD", or use a noun modifier.
- Prefer "of" phrases and noun modifiers. Split a long chain of "of" phrases into two sentences when it hides the subject.
- Use plain, common words when they are exact.
- Keep common field abbreviations as they are. Define an abbreviation at first use only where the venue asks for it or readers may not know it.
- Use the present tense for method descriptions and for established results. Some venues ask for the past tense in the methods section, so check the venue note before you choose this option.
- Write in prose. Use a list only for separate, parallel items, and only where the venue allows lists.

## Caption case

- Captions that are noun phrases: Title Case, with no final period.
- Captions that are full sentences: sentence case, with a final period.
- Apply one rule to every caption and table title in the scope. The venue's output profile overrides it.

## Formal Chinese article prose

- Use full-width Chinese punctuation (，。；：“”（）) in Chinese text. Keep half-width marks in formulas, code, and English terms.
- Replace personal feeling with an objective statement, such as the measured result from the record. Use a `[TODO: ...]` marker when no record exists.
- Use the current academic written register. Do not replace established wording with older or official forms, such as 拟 for 旨在 or 系 for 是, unless the researcher writes that way.
- Do not add an English term in brackets after a Chinese term, unless the venue asks for bilingual terms.
- Keep English technical names as the field uses them, such as Transformer, CNN, or few-shot. Keep established Chinese technical terms as they are.
- Avoid stacked 被-passives when the actor is known, and name the actor instead. Keep 被 when the actor is unknown.
