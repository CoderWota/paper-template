# Research Assessment Requirements

Confirmed guidance for idea development, originality checks, impact assessment, and mock review. Read only when one of these tasks is in scope.

- Do not require absolute originality or reject an idea only because similar work exists. Compare the closest inspected work by question, approach, conditions, results, and limits. Record search scope, source locations, and access limits; surface similarity alone does not establish duplication.
- Separate novelty, significance, feasibility, and support. Distinguish a meaningful contribution from a difference, and potential value from demonstrated value. Replications and negative results can matter without a methodological-novelty claim. Do not invent gaps, novelty, beneficiaries, or effects.
- Assess academic and social significance separately. Explain what knowledge, method, or evidence could improve; for social value, identify whose needs, a plausible path to benefit, and its conditions and limits. Label assumptions. Fit comparisons to the discipline; do not force experimental metrics on interpretive work.
- For important value claims, name evidence or comparisons that could strengthen or weaken them. Help develop immature ideas; where close work overlaps in question, approach, and value, explain the precise overlap and evidence-based options. The researcher decides whether to stop or change direction.
- Keep assessments in existing records. An assessment is not evidence and does not itself change research questions, claims, or idea status. Follow privacy and source-archiving rules; get confirmation for consequential choices and external transfers.

Scope: this project, in both clients, for idea development, originality checks, and impact assessment. These rules govern assessment, not manuscript findings or edits.

## Literature-grounded mock review in every discipline

- Rule: Ground originality and academic and societal significance in relevant field literature. Use the paper's references as discovery leads, verify support, and supplement close comparisons and contrary evidence where needed. Sciverse or authorized Agent internet search may be used; neither route alone is mandatory for mock review, and web search does not require Sciverse setup. This provider-choice exception applies to mock review, not unrelated literature-discovery tasks.
- Scope: `mock-review` assessments in every discipline, in both clients. Existing query-sharing, access, cost, and read-only boundaries still apply. This does not extend the humanities and social sciences societal-contribution threshold to other disciplines.

## Publication prospects under current journal standards

- Rule: The primary purpose of a named-target mock review is to estimate whether the supplied manuscript can ultimately be accepted at that journal through editorial screening, review, and normal feasible revisions. State the version, stage, and revision assumptions; do not assume unresolved analyses validate the findings or new evidence can be obtained. Lead with a coarse eventual-acceptance estimate and confidence, then give a separate forecast for the next identified editorial decision: first round for a new submission or the specified next round for a pending manuscript.
- Rule: Keep all six supplementary next-decision categories: 6 accept, 5 minor revision, 4 major revision, 3 revise and resubmit, 2 reject, 1 strong reject. Their coarse percentages total 100%, with manuscript- and venue-based reasons. Do not equate reviewer recommendations, next editor decisions, and final acceptance, or sum revision categories into final acceptance. Keep both forecasts consistent under the same submission route. They are subjective unless independently validated; do not derive them from scores, votes, journal-wide acceptance rates, journal metrics, or target-paper citations.
- Rule: Use current official journal criteria and current relevant literature by default, regardless of planned, pending, decided, or published status. A decided or published paper receives a clearly labeled current counterfactual. Only an explicit historical backtest changes the assessment date. Use official forms unchanged and report missing criteria. Without a target, give the general assessment rather than venue-specific odds. Journal selection and submission readiness retain their own no-odds scope.
- Scope: Publication-prospect `mock-review` assessments in both clients and every discipline. Reading/citation-only assessments need not forecast publication.

## Blind outcome comparison and calibration

- Rule: Keep known publication status, editorial outcomes, journal metrics, and target-paper citations from reviewers until findings and forecasts are locked; mask authorship where required and disclose exposure. Compare forecasts with verified outcomes for the matching version, venue, event/round, and assessment standard. Historical publication alone does not verify an initial decision or the outcome of a present-day counterfactual. A missing decision letter limits validation of that round, not the ability to assess current prospects.
- Rule: Use metrics and citations only for the post-review reception comparison, stating provider, year/version, and lookup date; do not change locked ratings after reveal. Use small sets as qualitative checks, include unsuccessful cases when available, and validate changes on independent cases before claiming calibrated probabilities.
- Scope: Mock-review outcome comparisons and calibration in both clients and all disciplines.

## Humanities and social sciences mock review

- Rule: Evaluate prior-work differences, theoretical or interpretive contribution, and societal contribution separately. Retain the project threshold of a supported path from a concrete social problem and affected people to the paper's added understanding and a potential benefit, with conditions and limits; actual policy adoption or intervention effects are not required. Societal contribution informs publication forecasts through its relevance to the target journal, article type, and claims. Report an unmet project threshold explicitly without silently treating it as every journal's rejection gate.
- Rule: Fit evidence standards to the question, research tradition, source access, and ethical constraints. Do not generally require natural experiments, random assignment, precise causal effects, large samples, statistical significance, or control of every confounder. Lack of causal identification or reasonable uncertainty alone does not justify a low score. Check explicit causal claims and assess what survives narrower conclusions; retain scrutiny of fabrication, invalid analysis, and decisive evidential gaps.
- Scope: Humanities and social sciences assessments through `mock-review`, in both clients. These requirements do not change other skills' thresholds, manuscript claims, or official venue forms.

"We cannot apply excessively strict data-analysis acceptance standards to humanities and social sciences; analysis is more difficult than in natural experiments, and we cannot expect precise causal effects."
