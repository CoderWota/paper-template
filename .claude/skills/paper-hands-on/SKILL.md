# Paper hands-on

Outcome: for newcomers, a usable directory tour and a clear entry into their first task; otherwise, progress toward the researcher's agreed goal, with inspectable evidence, research choices brought back for discussion, and authorized routine work connected through the existing records. The researcher directs the inquiry and final expression; the agent prepares comparisons, challenges assumptions, and carries the production and coordination. An interrupted session can continue from `state/current.md` and the linked records.

## Choose the entry before running a workflow

- **New to the workspace or unsure how to use it:** requests such as "first time here", "I do not know how to start", "where do files go?", or "explain this project" use [the beginner directory guide](directory-guide.md). Use its compact first-reply format; load detailed folder explanations only when asked. Complete the tour branch before Step 1; do not run the project-status, onboarding, environment, or credential workflows merely to explain the folders. A tour-only request ends there without writing state or requirements.
- **Continue or decide the next research step:** read [guided flow](guided-flow.md) and resume from the current records; do not repeat the beginner tour unless asked.
- **A named task, even from a beginner:** go directly to its specialist skill. A request to polish a paragraph is not permission to initialize a project or give a compulsory full tour. Explain a relevant folder briefly if useful.
- **Explicit project setup:** use `project-onboarding`. After a tour, pass along the user's actual answers and selected first task; do not ask them to repeat their material or give another directory lecture. A tour and a greeting alone do not authorize setup or a research task.

## How to guide

- For an orientation, use only the beginner branch. The steps and completion checks below concern an authorized research goal, not a tour.
- This skill selects and connects tasks; it never repeats their procedures. Each step is carried out exactly as the matching skill says, with its own checks.
- Work toward the requested outcome, not a fixed sequence of lessons. Explain each useful action briefly, carry it out, and continue through routine handoffs already covered by the request and confirmed choices. A progress update is not a request for permission. Ask only for missing judgments, material or access, and major choices.
- Help the researcher understand what the evidence permits: distinguish observations, interpretations, and hypotheses; surface competing explanations and evidence that could change the conclusion. Do not select a scientific conclusion just to keep the work moving.
- Never hand the researcher a technical chore: no copying between tools, no editing of records, no running commands, no keeping track of progress.
- A direct request for one task goes straight to its skill; the guided path is optional and never forces earlier stages.
