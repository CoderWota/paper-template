# Venue, contribution, and design context

Read before assigning reviewers or scoring. Use this context across disciplines and review purposes. It sets the basis for judgment; it does not add a score, weighted total, or acceptance forecast. Return the context in the review itself; do not create records for a read-only request.

## Venue fit

Check the target venue's current official aims, article types, review criteria, and relevant methodological or reporting requirements. Identify the intended readers and why the question, contribution, and practical or theoretical implications matter to them. Distinguish scope fit from the quality of the work. An out-of-scope paper may still be sound; a good fit does not resolve a defect.

When needed to establish a realistic comparison, inspect closely related articles of the same type in that venue or an appropriate field outlet. Report which methods and contribution types those examples use. Examples illustrate practice; they do not establish a universal threshold, an editorial preference, or why a particular paper was accepted. Official requirements take precedence over inferred practice. Without a target venue, use the stated audience and article type, not an assumed elite-journal standard. Missing venue evidence leaves fit unassessed without blocking other judgments.

## Concrete added knowledge

Compare the paper with the closest inspected studies by question, population or material, design, findings, and limits. State the incremental contribution as: **prior work established X; this paper adds or qualifies Y; that changes understanding or use because Z**. Use a compact comparison where it helps; neither a fixed paper count nor a fixed table is required.

A new setting or sample needs an explained payoff, such as testing transferability, revealing an excluded experience, separating outcomes previously combined, refining a boundary, or providing useful replication. No new theory or method is required when an empirical, interpretive, conceptual, or replication contribution fits the article. Identify genuinely overlapping results without treating any overlap as duplication. Separate the authors' advertised contribution from a narrower contribution the inspected work actually supports; narrowing a claim can preserve substantial value.

For retrospective evaluation, distinguish literature available at the time of the reviewed version from later work. Later studies can change current reference value but do not establish that an earlier novelty claim was false when made. Inadequate access leaves the relevant comparison unassessed; it does not establish a gap or lack of novelty.

## Design appropriate to claims and field practice

Use relevant methodological guidance and inspected studies to understand how comparable questions are studied, including the actual constraints on sampling, evidence, ethics, and access. Compare like questions and article types. A cross-sectional questionnaire should not be held to an intervention-effect standard when it claims bounded associations; a historical interpretation should be assessed through its sources and reasoning. An observed association can support an exploratory contribution without precise causal identification or measured real-world effects.

Then check the design independently against this paper's actual claims. Frequency of use or prior publication does not validate a method. Explain the specific consequence of any mismatch. Distinguish a flaw in the measurement, analysis, or reasoning from an ambitious interpretation that can be narrowed. Request longitudinal data, experiments, larger samples, new archives, or new proofs only when necessary for a contribution that the work needs to establish, not merely because they would improve a future study.

For each consequential requested change, state whether it is necessary for the retained contribution, necessary only for a stronger optional claim, or an optional extension. Keep optional work outside the reasons for downgrading the present study. Combine related issues into the minimum coherent revision plan and state which changes suffice; do not continue expanding requirements solely because further improvements are possible. A report inconsistency needs correction and an assessment of its reach; the absence of raw data or an author response during a review does not by itself make every conclusion unassessable. Use conditional treatment only when the unresolved issue is decisive for the overall recommendation. Preserve separate judgments for supported results and affected claims; do not assume either that a disputed result survives or that it invalidates independent evidence.

## Use in the review

Before the scores or overall judgment, give a short evidence-based statement of **venue fit, concrete added knowledge, and design adequacy** with the source basis and relevant access limits. Give each reviewer this context and its sources without a provisional verdict; reviewers can challenge the comparisons. Recheck all three in the meta-review. Test decisive objections against the manuscript's actual definitions, scope, and relevant responses or supplementary checks. Restate a misread objection if a supported concern remains; remove it if none does. Also check missed weaknesses in the proposed defense. Acknowledging a limitation does not alone resolve it. Explain which strengths justify retaining the work and which specific problems change the recommendation. These judgments do not replace the humanities and social sciences societal-contribution threshold or a venue's unchanged form.
