# Argument and impact guidance

Read this reference when a task assesses or changes the paper's contribution, claim strength, limitations, significance, or fit for a journal. A bounded sentence edit does not need it.

## Make the case persuasive

- State the problem or gap, the paper's contribution, and why the contribution matters to the relevant field or audience. For a journal submission, connect that value to the venue's scope and the questions its readers are likely to care about.
- Support the impact case with inspected literature, checked results, or clearly labeled reasoning about potential value. Explain the path from contribution to value; do not rely on generic adjectives, journal metrics, or an assumed reviewer reaction.
- Use clear, confident language for supported findings and a direct contribution statement. Avoid repetitive caveats that hide the paper's value. Place material limitations where they help readers understand the affected claim or use, and follow venue requirements.

## Keep claims accurate

- Represent original deductions, analyses, interpretations, and judgments through their stated premises, operations, criteria, and conditions. A derived claim can be supported by a sound argument; evaluate empirical premise support separately from inferential adequacy.
- Distinguish observed findings, interpretations, hypotheses, and possible future effects. Keep empirical, causal, novelty, generalization, and validation claims within what the inspected sources and project results support.
- Present potential impact as potential. Never imply that an untested use, unperformed comparison, unverified result, or future benefit has already been demonstrated. Do not invent evidence or conceal material negative findings.
- When a stronger claim needs more support, state the specific gap and how it affects the claim. Suggest a useful comparison, analysis, source, or clearer argument. The researcher decides changes to the main question or claim.
- A persuasive submission explains why reviewers should care; no wording can guarantee reviewer agreement or acceptance.
