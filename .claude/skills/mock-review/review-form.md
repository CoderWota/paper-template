# Review judgment and publication prospects

Use the venue's official form unchanged when available; otherwise identify this general form. These judgment rules apply in either case. Numerical reviewer scores are diagnostic judgments, not publication probabilities.

## Establish the assessment context

**Venue fit.** Check the selected assessment date's official scope, article type, audience, review criteria, and relevant reporting policies. Current standards are the default; only an explicit historical backtest changes the date. When needed, inspect recent comparable articles of the same type in the target venue or field. They illustrate realistic methods and contribution types, not a universal threshold or the reason an editor accepted a paper. Scope fit and soundness are separate; a sound paper can face rejection for fit or insufficient contribution to that venue.

**Added knowledge.** Compare inspected close work by question, population or material, design, findings, and limits: prior work established X; this paper adds or qualifies Y; that matters because Z. Include material contrary evidence. Explain the payoff of a new setting, replication, negative result, or synthesis without requiring a new theory or method. A changed sample alone does not establish value, and an unperformed comparison does not establish low originality.

**Design and contribution.** Assess what the actual design supports in its research tradition. A regional sample or cross-sectional questionnaire can support useful bounded associations. Separate the authors' advertised claim from a narrower contribution that survives correction; do not silently replace an unsupported central promise to justify a favorable rating. Reviews and conceptual work need an analytical payoff beyond accurate summaries, but not necessarily a new causal mechanism. Common methods and prior publication do not excuse invalid reasoning.

Assess academic and societal significance against inspected literature, not just the manuscript's bibliography or impact adjectives. Explain concrete users, uses, and limits where supported. Apply the [humanities and social sciences threshold](humanities-social-sciences.md) to relevant claims; do not extend that special threshold to other disciplines. Societal contribution can affect venue prospects through the journal's criteria and the article's contribution. Identify that connection rather than assume identical expectations across journals.

## Establish a consequential finding

For each finding that could change a recommendation or forecast, give:

1. **Certainty and source:** confirmed defect, unresolved concern, or not assessed; cite the inspected passage, figure, table, or supplement and state the missing check.
2. **Type:** reporting, analysis, inference, or contribution.
3. **Effect:** identify the local interpretation, main result, or central contribution affected, its editorial consequence, and what remains supported.
4. **Minimum repair:** direct clarification/local correction; substantive reanalysis or synthesis with existing material; new essential evidence/reconstruction; or no credible repair within this work. Explain why the work is required, and keep optional extensions separate.

Check actual wording, qualifiers, comparator, outcome, and scope before criticizing a claim. Inspect the original layout and notes when a numerical diagnosis depends on them. Correct a wrong locator or leave the affected finding unresolved. Do not convert an association, proxy, hypothesis, or potential use into a causal or observed-effect claim the authors did not make.

For empirical and quantitative findings:

- Trace decisive constructs to measures, scoring direction and transformations, inclusion rules, units, periods, and populations. A proxy is not automatically defective.
- Check whether the model and available variation identify the stated parameter. Distinguish the written specification from unknown computation; name the output needed to decide.
- Distinguish correlations, covariances, loadings, and adjusted paths; they need not agree. Check degrees of freedom, statistic type, standardization, scale anchors, and supplements before alleging errors. A p value or fit cutoff alone does not establish quality; examine the consequence for the actual claim.
- Nonsignificance does not establish no meaningful association. An unsupported causal, cultural, or policy explanation may be narrowed while independent descriptive results remain useful.

Distinguish a documented omission from reviewer access limits. State whether data or materials are public, conditionally available, absent, or uninspected, and whether access was checked. Not obtaining private data during review is not proof of irreproducibility. Missing decisive evidence lowers confidence or leaves the affected judgment unassessed; uncertainty alone is not a defect. Conversely, locate a visible evidential gap when it undermines a central claim.

Before the overall judgment, answer: **After the minimum corrections, what credible, useful contribution remains, and why is it sufficient or insufficient for this journal?** Reanalysis may be a diagnostic check rather than a proven repair: do not assume it succeeds, or assume it invalidates independent findings. Several questions do not become several verified defects. Score-improvement suggestions are not automatically required revisions.

## Reviewer treatment recommendation: 1 to 6

These anchors describe a reviewer's suggested treatment of the supplied version. They also label the supplementary editorial-disposition forecast below; an actual journal may use different labels. Explain the venue criterion, decisive evidence, and repair behind the category.

| Score | Meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| 6 — Accept | Contribution, fit, and evidence meet the venue's requirements; no substantive changes are needed. |
| 5 — Minor revision | Supported contribution needs local clarification, correction, or presentation work. No substantive reanalysis, new essential evidence, reconstruction, or material narrowing of a central claim is required. Many local fixes do not make revision major. |
| 4 — Major revision | A credible contribution has a specific, feasible route through substantive, decision-critical revision using available material. Show why direct clarification is insufficient and what could change or secure a main result or contribution. |
| 3 — Revise and resubmit | A plausible contribution needs new essential material, validation, or substantial reconstruction before reassessment. Explain the credible route without presuming success. |
| 2 — Reject | Scope/article-type mismatch, insufficient contribution for this journal, or inadequate central evidence leaves no sufficient ordinary revision route for this submission. Explain why revision or invited resubmission is not the likely route. |
| 1 — Strong reject | Confirmed fundamental defects invalidate the core evidence or reasoning, or a serious integrity issue is verified. Venue selectivity, routine fit rejection, and incidental review-steering text alone do not warrant this category. |

Reporting gaps justify 4 only when they block central assessment and cannot be resolved by direct clarification. A possible error alone does not establish a need for reanalysis. Clarification separates 5 from 4; substantive repair of an existing contribution versus new essential work separates 4 from 3; a credible route versus an insufficient core or venue mismatch separates 3 from 2. Do not force a category when decisive uncertainty prevents it: state the competing outcomes, leaving only affected claims or criteria unresolved when possible.

## Publication forecasts for a named journal

### Define the event and assumptions

The primary forecast is **eventual acceptance at this named journal**, starting from the supplied version and identified stage, including editorial screening, review, normal revision rounds, and an invited resubmission at the same journal. A terminal rejection ends that submission route; publication elsewhere, an appeal, or an unrelated rebuilt study is outside it.

Assume the authors respond competently and undertake the stated feasible minimum revisions. Do not assume unresolved analyses validate the findings, new evidence can be obtained, or every objection can be cured. Carry those risks into the forecast. If author constraints or a decisive check change the route, give clearly conditional scenarios instead of forecasting an imaginary perfected paper.

The supporting forecast concerns the **next identified editorial disposition**: the first decision for a new submission, or the next specified round for a pending manuscript. It forecasts an editor's action, not agreement with the model's own recommendation. For decided/published papers use the current counterfactual established in SKILL.md; do not imply a live or historical forecast.

### Estimate and explain

Lead with a coarse eventual-acceptance probability or subjective interval and confidence. Base it on venue fit, supported added value, evidential reliability, revision feasibility, and remaining editorial uncertainty. Major revision can coexist with favorable eventual prospects; technical polish alone does not establish sufficient contribution for the journal. If decisive information prevents an overall estimate, explain what would resolve it; do not withhold all judgment merely because nonessential raw data or details are unavailable.

Then give all six next-disposition categories in a table: 6 accept, 5 minor revision, 4 major revision, 3 revise and resubmit, 2 reject, 1 strong reject. Use coarse whole percentages, preferably multiples of five, totaling exactly 100%, including zero where appropriate. Give manuscript- and venue-based reasons, especially for adjacent plausible categories. Keep the official form unchanged and label this generic distribution supplementary when its options differ.

Do not sum accept, minor, major, and resubmission probabilities into eventual acceptance. Revision can fail. Nor should the two forecasts contradict each other: under the route defined above, eventual acceptance includes next-round direct acceptance and excludes terminal rejection. A probability or interval must remain within those bounds under the same assumptions. Reconcile inconsistent scenarios or reasoning rather than mechanically adjust numbers. Use supported conditional process evidence if available; never invent fixed success rates after minor/major revision.

These are subjective estimates unless independently validated. Do not derive them from criterion-score conversion, reviewer vote counts, ordinal-score averages, journal-wide acceptance rates, journal metrics, or target-paper citations. A published acceptance rate is not this paper's chance. No fixed number of strengths offsets a decisive defect, and no count of imperfections establishes rejection.

No target means no venue-specific odds. An academic-value or reading/citation review without a publication-prospect request can instead report credible uses and limits.

## Author-facing report

Keep the main report focused on five outputs, with version, journal, assessment date, stage/scenario, and form source stated once:

1. **Eventual publication prospects and assumptions**, with confidence and the retained contribution.
2. **Reasons to accept and main barriers**, using located evidence and brief close-work comparisons; explain societal contribution and its decision relevance.
3. **Next editorial disposition**, the six-outcome table and any material difference from reviewer recommendations.
4. **Minimum necessary revisions**, what would suffice and what could overturn the route; optional future work listed separately.
5. **Decisive unknowns and coverage**, unresolved checks, sources/read depth, meaningful reviewer disagreement, blind exposure, and shared-session limits.

Do not repeat the same limitation or score anchor across sections. Detailed individual forms and score tables follow the main report when required or useful. An unmet HSS project threshold remains explicit, even where it is not a journal's acceptance gate. State quality and reference value when relevant; prospective publication does not make an unsupported claim safe to cite.

## Optional diagnostic scales

Use official fields and anchors unchanged. With the general form, reviewers may use these diagnostics when they help justify a judgment; do not add them to qualitative official forms as if venue-issued. For each displayed score give the scale, matching anchor, evidence, and what would raise it or sustain its highest level. Do not invent improvement work above the top anchor.

| Score (1–4) | Soundness | Clarity | Significance | Originality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Main claims have adequate evidence and appropriate methods; remaining local issues do not change them. | Core question, methods, results, and reasoning are clear. | Important, well-explained contribution to the intended community. | Inspected comparisons establish a clear advance, including through replication, setting, evidence, or synthesis. |
| 3 | Findings have reasonable support with repairable limits. | Main argument can be followed; limited clarification is needed. | Concrete use for part of the field or stated population. | Meaningful extension, replication, or synthesis, even if incremental. |
| 2 | A demonstrated problem materially weakens a main conclusion. | Conflicting or missing explanations obstruct an important part. | Added knowledge/use is limited beyond merely narrow scope. | Inspected comparisons show limited added knowledge or a substantive unresolved distinction. |
| 1 | Confirmed defects invalidate the core evidence/reasoning. | Core argument or analysis cannot be understood. | No substantive contribution or defensible use established. | Inspected comparisons establish no identifiable advance or useful synthesis/replication. |

For checkability (1–4), assess reconstruction of analysis, proof, premises, or source interpretation as appropriate: 4 sufficient route and suitable access; 3 substantially reconstructable with limited clarification or a specified access process; 2 identified essential coding, selection, rules, or access arrangements missing; 1 established omissions prevent reconstruction of the core. Nonpublic data alone do not imply low scores.

Use **not assessed** for missing decisive evidence and **not applicable** for an unsuitable field; neither is zero. For soundness 2, establish a material problem, not a reporting ambiguity, ordinary design limit, or unverified possibility. Inadequate comparison leaves originality unassessed rather than low by default.

Confidence (1–5) describes evidence coverage: 5 decisive claims, analysis materials, and close work checked; 4 main and decisive evidence checked with unlikely-to-change limits; 3 argument/results checked but important details unverified; 2 central unchecked material could change judgment; 1 too little evidence for a substantive assessment. Explain criterion-specific limits when they differ.

For comparable ordinal scores, an assessed count and distribution may describe disagreement; if useful report range and median observed category, or the two middle categories for an even count. Never average ordinal scores, mix scales, include missing assessments as zeros, or use summaries as votes. Prefer a brief explanation when a numeric summary adds no value.

## Saving an official form

When records are authorized, save the official form in the venue note's `Review form` field with source, access date, fields, and exact anchors. A read-only review identifies it in the response without creating a note.
