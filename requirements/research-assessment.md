# Research Assessment Requirements

Confirmed guidance for idea development, originality checks, impact assessment, and mock review. Read only when one of these tasks is in scope.

- Do not require absolute originality or reject an idea only because similar work exists. Compare the closest inspected work by question, approach, conditions, results, and limits. Record search scope, source locations, and access limits; surface similarity alone does not establish duplication.
- Separate novelty, significance, feasibility, and support. Distinguish a meaningful contribution from a difference, and potential value from demonstrated value. Replications and negative results can matter without a methodological-novelty claim. Do not invent gaps, novelty, beneficiaries, or effects.
- Assess academic and social significance separately. Explain what knowledge, method, or evidence could improve; for social value, identify whose needs, a plausible path to benefit, and its conditions and limits. Label assumptions. Fit comparisons to the discipline; do not force experimental metrics on interpretive work.
- For important value claims, name evidence or comparisons that could strengthen or weaken them. Help develop immature ideas; where close work overlaps in question, approach, and value, explain the precise overlap and evidence-based options. The researcher decides whether to stop or change direction.
- Keep assessments in existing records. An assessment is not evidence and does not itself change research questions, claims, or idea status. Follow privacy and source-archiving rules; get confirmation for consequential choices and external transfers.

Scope: this project, in both clients, for idea development, originality checks, and impact assessment. These rules govern assessment, not manuscript findings or edits.

Confirmed 2026-10-30: "We do not pursue absolute originality. Do not end the discussion merely because someone has already done something similar. Explain what we add, why the difference matters, and how to verify its value. Do not manufacture novelty to retain an idea." "Consider academic and social significance." "Project-level long-term rules." The researcher requested implementation of the plan to integrate these principles into the existing repository structure.

## Literature-grounded mock review in every discipline

- Rule: Ground originality and academic and societal significance in relevant field literature. Use the paper's references as discovery leads, verify support, and supplement close comparisons and contrary evidence where needed. Sciverse or authorized Agent internet search may be used; neither route alone is mandatory for mock review, and web search does not require Sciverse setup. This provider-choice exception applies to mock review, not unrelated literature-discovery tasks.
- Scope: `mock-review` assessments in every discipline, in both clients. Existing query-sharing, access, cost, and read-only boundaries still apply. This does not extend the humanities and social sciences societal-contribution threshold to other disciplines.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "To assess academic and real-world significance, use related literature in the field; Sciverse or Agent internet search may be used." "You can also use the paper's own references as search leads." "This requirement applies to all disciplines."

## Targeted mock review and six-outcome estimates

- Rule: For a submission-oriented mock review with a named target journal, inspect current official scope, article-type, reviewer, and submission criteria, cite the sources and access dates, and assess venue fit separately from academic contribution and overall quality. When the paper's submission status is uncertain, use supplied material and authorized public records without treating the absence of a record as proof that it was not submitted. State the assumption or scenario for any estimate; if status remains unknown, make the estimate explicitly conditional (for example, if this version were submitted to the named journal now). Do not present it as a live forecast of a decision that may already have occurred.
- Rule: Include all six simulated treatment outcomes in the final report: 6 accept, 5 minor revision, 4 major revision, 3 revise and resubmit, 2 reject, 1 strong reject. Give a coarse probability for each, make the six total 100%, and explain the manuscript evidence and journal criteria behind each estimate, including why adjacent outcomes differ. Keep this subjective distribution separate from the venue's official form and scores. Do not derive it from journal acceptance rates, reviewer votes, or criterion-score conversion. If the target or a final recommendation cannot be assessed, say why and use only a clearly labeled conditional estimate where appropriate.
- Scope: Submission-oriented `mock-review` assessments with a named target, in both clients and every discipline. Journal-selection notes and submission-readiness assessments do not produce this distribution.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-31; "When reviewing a paper whose submission status may be uncertain, assess it against the planned journal's subject scope and acceptance criteria. In the final result, give probabilities for all six outcomes and explain the reasons for them."

## Humanities and social sciences mock review

- Rule: Evaluate prior-work differences, theoretical or interpretive contribution, and societal contribution separately. Require a supported path from a concrete social problem and affected people to the paper's added understanding and a potential benefit, with conditions and limits. Actual policy adoption or intervention effects are not required. Identify this as the skill's assessment requirement, separate from a venue's official criteria.
- Rule: Fit evidence standards to the question, research tradition, source access, and ethical constraints. Do not generally require natural experiments, random assignment, precise causal effects, large samples, statistical significance, or control of every confounder. Lack of causal identification or reasonable uncertainty alone does not justify a low score. Check explicit causal claims and assess what survives narrower conclusions; retain scrutiny of fabrication, invalid analysis, and decisive evidential gaps.
- Scope: Humanities and social sciences assessments through `mock-review`, in both clients. These requirements do not change other skills' thresholds, manuscript claims, or official venue forms.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "Set it as a necessary threshold." "A clear pathway is sufficient." "We cannot apply excessively strict data-analysis acceptance standards to humanities and social sciences; analysis is more difficult than in natural experiments, and we cannot expect precise causal effects." The researcher explicitly requested implementation of the plan incorporating these requirements.
