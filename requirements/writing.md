# Writing Requirements

Active, researcher-confirmed writing rules and terminology for the manuscript. Each rule states its scope (the whole manuscript, a section, or an output profile) and, where helpful, an example or an allowed exception.

## Researcher control of manuscript tone

- Rule: Follow the researcher's confirmed tone and style preferences for each scope. Communication rules and general style advice do not override them. Do not impose short sentences, common words, or ASD-STE100 on manuscript prose, and do not infer tone preferences.
- Scope: Drafting, revision, and translation of the manuscript. The researcher confirms the scope of each tone preference.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "We also need to consider the tone of AI-written papers. It must follow the researcher's preferences and must not be overridden by absolute rules."

## Manuscript source archive

- Rule: Before a paper is cited or used to inform manuscript claims, background, interpretation, or structure, archive the original document for that specific version in `references/originals/<source-id>/`. An abstract, search snippet, service-only text, search record, source note, or bibliographic metadata alone does not count as an archive. If the original cannot be obtained through an authorized route, do not cite or use the paper as a manuscript source; report the access gap.
- Scope: Whole manuscript.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "The papers we actually cite, or papers we refer to in writing, must be archived in the project."

## Citation source preference

- Rule: Prefer the peer-reviewed, formally published version of a work for manuscript citations when its identity and support for the cited claim are verified.
- Scope: Whole manuscript.
- Exceptions: An arXiv preprint may be cited only when no published version can be found in the sources checked for the task. If a published version is found but its original cannot be archived, report an access gap; do not substitute an archived preprint.
- Presentation: When an arXiv preprint is cited, identify its preprint status in the bibliography entry or source record, never in manuscript body text.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "We should mainly use published articles as citation sources; arXiv is a preprint server and has less authority than a journal." "Clearly identify a preprint outside the manuscript body."
