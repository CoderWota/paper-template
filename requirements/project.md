## Constructive assessment of originality and significance

- Rule: Do not require absolute originality or reject a direction merely because similar work exists. First understand and try to develop the researcher's idea, while checking overlap, counterexamples, and contrary evidence. This does not require retaining an unsupported claim.
  - Compare the closest inspected work in its core question, key approach, conditions of use, results, and limitations. Names, short descriptions, and surface similarities do not establish duplication. State the search scope, source locators, and access limits; keep judgments preliminary when coverage is limited.
  - Distinguish a difference from a meaningful contribution, and a potential contribution from demonstrated value. Explain what the idea adds or advances, why that matters, and what evidence supports the explanation. Replications and negative results can have value without a claim of methodological novelty. Do not exaggerate a superficial difference or invent novelty to preserve an idea.
  - Assess academic and social significance separately. For academic significance, explain what understanding, explanation, method, or evidence could improve and what comparison would support that advance. For social significance, identify whose needs could be addressed, the path to benefit, its conditions, costs, and limits. Either form of significance may remain unclear; a paper need not demonstrate both. Do not invent beneficiaries or actual effects.
  - For each key value claim, propose a concrete way to test it: the relevant comparison or evidence, what would support the claim, and what would weaken it. Fit the approach to the discipline; do not force experimental metrics on conceptual, historical, or interpretive work.
  - When an idea is immature, suggest specific ways to develop, refine, or deepen it and how to check their value. When its core question, approach, and main value substantially overlap with prior work, identify the works and the precise overlap, then suggest evidence-based adjustments. If no substantive distinction can be supported, say so and, when warranted, recommend stopping the current route with reasons. The researcher decides consequential changes.
  - Use the existing skills: `idea-deepening` for originality and development, `impact-assessment` for significance, literature skills for retrieval and source checks, and `research-planning` for authorized validation work. Reuse inspected comparisons and evidence. Repeat or extend a search only when a relevant evidence gap requires it. Existing privacy and query-sharing requirements still apply; this rule authorizes no external transfer.
  - Keep retained ideas and comparisons in `ideas/`, search records in `references/searches/`, and source evidence with a real archived source ID in `references/notes/`. Service-only material stays in its search record with its access limits. Before reusing a comparison or assessment in manuscript content, apply the archive and citation-version requirements in [writing.md](writing.md); an evaluation record does not replace the original source. Save impact assessments in `research/impact.md` only when asked. Put authorized validation plans in `research/plan.md` and actual research results in `research/results/`. Reuse existing layouts and statuses; introduce no separate evaluation system or score. An assessment does not itself change the research question, manuscript claims, or idea status. Record a consequential research choice only after the researcher confirms that choice.
- Scope: This project, long term, in both clients, for idea development, originality checks, and impact assessment. These rules govern evaluation; they do not establish findings or authorize manuscript changes.
- Confirmed: 2026-10-30; "We do not pursue absolute originality. Do not end the discussion merely because someone has already done something similar. Explain what we add, why the difference matters, and how to verify its value. Do not manufacture novelty to retain an idea." "Consider academic and social significance." "Project-level long-term rules." The researcher explicitly requested implementation of the plan to integrate these principles into the existing repository structure.
